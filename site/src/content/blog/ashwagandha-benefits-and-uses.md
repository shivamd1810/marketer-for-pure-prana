Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) is one of the most researched and widely used herbs in Ayurvedic medicine. Often called "Indian Ginseng," this powerful adaptogen has been used for over 3,000 years to help the body manage stress, boost energy, and promote overall vitality.

In recent years, ashwagandha has gained enormous popularity in the West — and for good reason. With over 300 published studies examining its effects, it's one of the most evidence-backed herbs in the natural supplement world.

## What Is Ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha is a small evergreen shrub native to India, the Middle East, and parts of Africa. Its name comes from Sanskrit: *ashva* (horse) and *gandha* (smell), referring both to the root's distinct aroma and the traditional belief that it imparts the strength and vitality of a horse.

In Ayurveda, ashwagandha is classified as a *Rasayana* — a rejuvenating herb that promotes longevity, vitality, and happiness. It's considered one of the most important herbs in the entire Ayurvedic pharmacopeia.

## Proven Benefits of Ashwagandha

### 1. Stress and Cortisol Management

Ashwagandha's most well-known benefit is its ability to help the body adapt to stress. As an adaptogen, it helps regulate cortisol — the body's primary stress hormone.

A landmark study published in the *Indian Journal of Psychological Medicine* found that participants taking ashwagandha root extract experienced a significant reduction in perceived stress scores and serum cortisol levels compared to the placebo group.

**How it works:** Ashwagandha modulates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, helping to normalize the body's stress response rather than simply masking symptoms.

**Dosage:** Most studies use 300-600 mg of root extract daily, typically standardized to 5% withanolides.

**With food:** Taking ashwagandha with a meal improves absorption and reduces the chance of stomach sensitivity.

## Who Should Avoid Ashwagandha?

While ashwagandha is generally well-tolerated, it's not recommended for:

- Pregnant or breastfeeding women (consult your doctor)
- People with autoimmune conditions (may stimulate immune activity)
- Those taking thyroid medication (may affect thyroid hormone levels)
- People on sedatives or anti-anxiety medications (may enhance effects)

Always consult a healthcare provider before starting any new supplement, especially if you take prescription medications.
